# Elasticsearch: per-bucket `top_hits` state not counted by circuit breakers, reported to cause a data-node OOM

- Source issue: https://github.com/elastic/elasticsearch/issues/162932 (open at check time; labels: >bug, :Analytics/Aggregations, Team:Analytics, Team:Search Foundations; opened 2026-10-21 23:04 UTC; 3 comments)

## Problem
The reporter (who is described in an automated comment as a member of the Elasticsearch team) says a `terms` aggregation with `size: 10000` and 23 sibling `top_hits` sub-aggregations (each `size: 1`, sorted on a `long` field, loading `fields`) ran a 4 GB search node out of heap on Elasticsearch 9.6. Per the report, the request circuit breaker read 35.7 MB when the node died, while a heap dump held about 2.28 GB of `TopHitsAggregator` state from two concurrent shard requests, plus 457 MB held by sub-search contexts. They say the parent breaker tripped in bursts for about 30 minutes before the OOM, and that repeated full GCs freed little.

## The reporter's explanation
They say `TopHitsAggregator` keeps one collector per bucket and, on a large segment, one live leaf collector per bucket at the same time. With a field sort, each leaf collector opens its own doc-values reader, about 5.2 KB per bucket for each `top_hits` by their estimate. They say almost none of that is charged to a breaker, so memory grows with buckets times `top_hits` without the breaker seeing it. The 457 MB part is said to match the earlier issue #158722, which was fixed in PR #159231, and the affected build did not include that fix.

## What this does not tell you
- It does not say any particular cluster is at risk. The report involves a very large `terms` size, many sibling `top_hits`, and a 4 GB node.
- It is one team member's report and an automated hypothesis. Neither has been confirmed by a reproduced test.
- Nothing here is guidance for changing queries or breaker settings on a production cluster.
